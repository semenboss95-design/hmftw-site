How Much For The Whip

Pick a make, model and years and see how many such lots passed their sale date at US salvage auctions, state by state, and the auction's own retail estimate for them, with the sample size and window next to every number. What such cars sold for and what they are listed for is not here yet: the tiles say why instead of showing a number.

How we count

  1. Sold for (hammer): not yet. It is to come from public auction-result archives, as the winning bid without fees; no archive price has passed our checks so far, so the tile says "no verified sale prices yet" instead of a number.
  2. Asking prices. Private sellers and dealers: not yet. No listings source has cleared our legal check, so nothing is read and nothing is stored. The listing tiles say this in words and never show zero.
  3. Spread is asking minus (hammer + auction fees), before broker fee, storage, transport, title and repair. It is shown only where both sides exist.

Lots on upcoming sales, by state

We count lots that the last successful collection saw and whose sale has not happened yet. Right now there are 7,356 of them in 389 model and state pairs. n=7,356, 1-day window to 2026-09-26, US

This is not "how many such cars there are in a state": counted are lots with a sale date up to and including Oct 2, 2026, which is as far ahead as the source publishes right now. Past that date the source has not published a schedule yet; it does not mean there will be no cars.
